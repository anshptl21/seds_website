    SEDS SEDS Solar Sail

    We are Texas A&M SEDS Solar Sail (TAMU SSS)—a student-led project under the Texas A&M chapter of SEDS, and we are building a satellite that will sail in space using nothing but sunlight. While this may sound like science fiction, solar sailing is a technology that has already been successfully demonstrated several times in space. It is not mature enough yet for widespread use, but we at TAMU SSS aim to help change that. As a student project, we believe we are uniquely positioned to take risks and develop bold innovations that will advance the field of solar sailing and realize its potential for applications such as Earth observation, orbital debris mitigation, interplanetary missions, and more.

    SOLARIS I

    After completing a full year of in-depth research and expert outreach, we decided to pursue a novel sail concept that utilizes shape-memory alloy (SMA) deployment to produce lighter, more efficient, and more reliable solar sails. Since then, we have been conducting R&D on our concept and working towards the launch of Solaris I: a small-scale solar sail mission which will deploy from the ISS and demonstrate our novel SMA deployment technology.

    Check out our CONOPS to learn more about the mission and how we strive to test our prototype in space!
